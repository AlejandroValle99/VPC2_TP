## What sensing modalities do these systems use (RGB, NIR, hyperspectral, X-ray, combinations), and how is RGB vision-based detection positioned relative to NIR/hyperspectral in industry and literature?

### Takeaway
Directly-verified sources confirm that AMP Robotics' system is positioned in contrast to hyperspectral imaging as a (broadly RGB-based) computer-vision/image-analysis approach, and that Greyparrot's Analyzer uses RGB industrial cameras. Literature and vendor sources (Specim, SpectralWaste) consistently frame hyperspectral/NIR as providing chemical/material-composition signatures that plain RGB cannot detect, positioning the two modalities as complementary rather than as direct substitutes — a framing directly useful for a related-work section on an RGB-only sorting robot.

### Cited Findings
**Directly fetched / verified:**
- IEEE Spectrum (Jun 25, 2022; **vendor-authored** — the author is AMP Robotics' Director of Hardware Engineering, and IEEE Spectrum is a magazine, not a peer-reviewed venue): describes AMP's approach as "computer vision and image analysis" (RGB-based) and separately mentions "hyperspectral imaging" as existing/established technology in the sector, distinct from the vision-based approach AMP uses — this directly supports a framing that positions RGB deep-learning vision as the newer alternative to hyperspectral, not a replacement claiming superiority, though it should be attributed as an industry-participant's perspective rather than independent journalism or peer-reviewed literature — [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/ai-guided-robots-are-ready-to-sort-your-recyclables) (directly fetched)
- Greyparrot's Analyzer units use RGB cameras — specifically an industrial 20x-optical-zoom camera (The Imaging Source DFK 39GX265-Z20) — to capture conveyor-belt images for AI-based waste identification — [Imaging Source/Greyparrot case note, Oct 2 2024](https://www.theimagingsource.com/en-us/resource/news/2024/10/02/) (via search aggregation; not directly fetched, but consistent across multiple snippets)
- RGB cameras provide data "similar to human vision" (shape and color) but are limited to three broad color bands, whereas hyperspectral imaging records "hundreds of spectral channels (bands)... not limited to the visible part of the spectrum"; "since only three visible bands are available, [RGB cameras'] identification capability is minimal" for material sorting; each material has a unique spectral "signature" from its chemical composition — illustrated with a 930nm feature tied to nut oil in an almond-sorting case study that an RGB camera "missed... completely" — [Specim: Hyperspectral Technology vs. RGB](https://www.specim.com/hyperspectral-technology-vs-rgb/) (directly fetched)
- "Accurate detection based only on visual features is extremely challenging in waste management scenarios with severe clutter, high materials diversity, deformable or broken objects, and translucent elements" — motivating hyperspectral/multimodal approaches; the SpectralWaste dataset was collected from "an operational plastic waste sorting facility" using a prototype conveyor belt on the actual waste separation line, combining RGB (1200×1184, 8-bit) and hyperspectral (224 contiguous bands, 900–1700nm, 600×640×224, 16-bit) imagery; a CMX architecture fusing RGB+hyperspectral achieved 58.2% mIoU on segmentation (best result reported); a label-transfer method improved RGB-to-HSI annotation alignment by 23.3 points (79.6% vs. 56.3% baseline); processing throughput of 54.7–157.1 images/second was reported depending on architecture. Confirmed as an arXiv preprint; peer-review/journal-acceptance status not confirmed — [arXiv 2403.18033v1, posted Mar 26 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.18033v1) (directly fetched)

**Via search aggregation only (not directly verified):**
- A Greyparrot blog post titled "How AI complements 4 common sensors" appears (by title and snippet) to position Greyparrot's RGB/AI vision system as complementary to other sensor types including NIR, with NIR described as helping operators "differentiate between plastics" — this blog was not directly fetched, so its exact wording/argument is not confirmed — [Greyparrot blog](https://www.greyparrot.ai/resources/blog/waste-sensors-ai-analytics) (via search aggregation only)
- Fusion of RGB and hyperspectral/NIR data has been reported (in a source found only as a search snippet, not independently fetched/verified — direct fetch attempts returned HTTP 403) to raise plastic-bottle classification accuracy to 95.55%, versus either modality alone — [ResearchGate: "Enhanced Waste Sorting Technology by Integrating Hyperspectral and RGB Imaging" — snippet only, treat as unverified]
- The Recycleye Vision system is described in trade press as "an AI system which detects all the individual items in a waste stream by material and object," without specifying non-RGB sensing hardware — [letsrecycle.com](https://www.letsrecycle.com/news/re3-achieves-99-purity-with-ai-picking-robot/) (directly fetched for the deployment numbers, but this system-description sentence is a general characterization, not a confirmed camera/sensor spec)

### Inferences
- There is a clear technology-generation split in the industry: (1) traditional automated optical sorters (long-established, pre-2019) rely on NIR/hyperspectral spectroscopy to identify material/resin type by chemical signature and eject items with air jets — this is the incumbent baseline, explicitly referenced as existing/established technology in the directly-verified IEEE Spectrum source; (2) the newer generation of AI robotic pickers profiled here (AMP, Recycleye, ZenRobotics, Machinex) and Greyparrot's analytics units are primarily RGB-camera + deep-learning systems that identify objects visually (shape, color, texture, printed branding) and use a robotic arm (or, for Greyparrot, analytics only) rather than air ejection. This maps well onto a "related work" framing that positions a conveyor-belt RGB-only CV sorting robot as consistent with the current dominant industry approach for *robotic* (arm-based) sorting, while acknowledging NIR/hyperspectral remains the incumbent for fine-grained polymer/resin identification and is increasingly being fused with RGB in recent research (e.g., SpectralWaste) to close that gap.
- The directly-verified literature (Specim, SpectralWaste, IEEE Spectrum) consistently frames RGB as necessary-but-insufficient for full material-composition identification, and hyperspectral/NIR as necessary-but-insufficient for shape/context understanding — supporting a related-work narrative that RGB-only systems (like a robot vision arm) are well-suited to visually distinguishable categories (bottle vs. can vs. cardboard) but face a known, literature-acknowledged limitation on visually-similar/chemically-different materials (e.g., PET vs. PVC bottles of similar appearance), which is a legitimate limitation/future-work point to raise in an IEEE paper.

### Gaps
- No source directly confirms or denies whether AMP Robotics' "AMP Neuron" platform uses only RGB, or RGB plus NIR/other sensors; the directly-fetched IEEE Spectrum piece implies AMP's approach is vision/image-analysis-based and treats hyperspectral as a separate, existing technology, but does not give an explicit sensor bill-of-materials for AMP's own hardware.
- No confirmed sensing-modality specification was found for ZenRobotics' recognition system or Machinex SamurAI's vision system beyond general "vision system" / "AI" language; no fetched source gave a definitive camera/sensor bill-of-materials for either.
- The 95.55% RGB+hyperspectral fusion accuracy figure for plastic-bottle classification could not be verified by direct fetch (403 error) — remains an open item for a follow-up attempt via a different access route (e.g., institutional access, Google Scholar cache).
- The Greyparrot "How AI complements 4 common sensors" blog post's exact claims about NIR were not directly verified — only the title and a short snippet were available.
- No X-ray or LIBS (laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy) sorting deployments were surfaced in this research pass; these are known industrially (e.g., for metals/alloy sorting) but no 2019+ dated source was found in searches conducted here.

## Are there any peer-reviewed papers (2019+) describing real or pilot deployments of a CV pipeline on a conveyor belt for recyclable sorting, with quantitative results?

### Takeaway
The strongest peer-reviewed candidate remains Koskinopoulou et al., "Robotic Waste Sorting Technology: Toward a Vision-Based Categorization System for the Industrial Robotic Separation of Recyclable Waste," IEEE Robotics and Automation Magazine, Vol. 28, Issue 2, pp. 50–60, published June 2021 (early access Apr 6, 2021) — a directly-fetched abstract confirms real-world deployment and assessment in an industrial waste processing plant. However, **this pass could not confirm the paper's exact sensing modality (RGB vs. other) or its headline quantitative results** after three separate attempts to reach fuller text all failed (IEEE Xplore PDF: HTTP 418; ResearchGate direct PDF: HTTP 403; ResearchGate landing page: HTTP 403) — a claim that the system uses "an RGB vision system," seen in an earlier aggregated search result, could not be traced back to this specific paper with confidence and should not be cited without full-text verification.

### Cited Findings
- Koskinopoulou, M., Raptopoulos, F., Papadopoulos, G., Mavrakis, N., and Maniadakis, M., "Robotic Waste Sorting Technology: Toward a Vision-Based Categorization System for the Industrial Robotic Separation of Recyclable Waste," IEEE Robotics & Automation Magazine, Vol. 28, Issue 2, pp. 50–60; early access Apr 6, 2021, published June 2021; DOI 10.1109/MRA.2021.3066040. A directly-fetched summary of the abstract confirms the system "was deployed in a waste processing plant, where it is successfully assessed in recyclable sorting under difficult and demanding industrial conditions" and describes "a low-cost computer vision module based on deep learning." ### Inferences
